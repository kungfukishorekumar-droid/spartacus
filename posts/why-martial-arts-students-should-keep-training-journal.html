Why Martial Arts Students Should Keep a Training Journal

Five minutes with a notebook after class doubles what you keep from the hour on the mat. The training journal, explained.

Spartacus Martial ArtsBy Kishore Kumar27 Sept 2025 · 09:30 PMUpdated 13 Oct 20264 min read
Why Martial Arts Students Should Keep a Training Journal

Quick Answer

A training journal captures corrections, wins and questions while fresh — turning vague classes into tracked progress. Three lines after each session is enough to compound learning dramatically.

My old competition notebooks are falling apart, and they are among my most valuable possessions. Corrections from my coaches, lessons from competitions, things that worked and things that did not — they all live in those pages. Here is why your training deserves the same.

Classes evaporate; journals condense

You receive many details in each session: corrections, cues, small breakthroughs. Untracked, most of them fade by dinner. A few written lines catch the essentials before they escape.

The three-line format

  • Today's correction — what the coach fixed. ("Heel down on the roundhouse.")
  • Today's win — what improved, however small. ("Held horse stance for 60 seconds.")
  • Today's question — what to ask or work on next class. ("Why does my side kick lose balance?")

It takes about three minutes.

What else to record

  • Attendance — a simple tick for every class
  • How you felt — energy, sleep, mood
  • Home practice — what and how long
  • Goals — for the month and the next grading
  • Competition notes — what worked, what to change

The compound effect

Students who keep a journal walk into each class with a plan instead of just turning up. Over a year, that difference is enormous — same classes, far more learning. You also start to see patterns: which sessions went well, what helps you perform, what drains you.

The motivational archive

On flat days, reading your month-one entries is powerful: you have proof of how far you have come. Progress you can flip through beats progress you must remember. Read why consistency creates champions.

For competitors

Before competitions, reading your journal reminds you of months of documented work — evidence that you are prepared. After competitions, it captures lessons while they are fresh. Read how to build confidence before competition.

Paper or phone?

Whichever you will actually use. Paper is distraction-free and satisfying to look back through. A notes app is always with you. The best journal is the one you keep writing.

Can kids keep training journals?

Yes. Younger children can draw a picture of something they learnt, or put a sticker on a chart. Older children can use a simplified version: "One thing I learnt, one thing I did well." Parents can help by asking the questions. Read how parents can support practice at home.

What if I miss journalling for weeks?

Just start again with today's entry. Do not try to catch up on everything you missed. A journal with gaps is still far more useful than no journal.

Beyond martial arts

Reflective writing is a habit used by athletes, musicians and professionals in many fields. Learning it in martial arts helps with studies and work too. It is one of the mental skills we encourage alongside physical training — read why athletes need mindset training.
