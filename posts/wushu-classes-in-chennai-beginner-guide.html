What is Wushu?

"Wushu" literally means "martial art" in Chinese. Today the word usually refers to the modern sport version of Chinese martial arts, which has two main branches:

  • Taolu (forms) — choreographed routines that show speed, power, balance, jumps and precision. Routines exist for barehand styles such as Changquan (long fist) and Nanquan (southern fist), and for weapons such as broadsword, straight sword, staff and spear.
  • Sanda (also called Sanshou) — the full-contact combat side, combining punches, kicks and throws under competition rules.

Most beginners start with the fundamentals shared by both branches: stances, footwork, basic strikes and kicks, and conditioning. Together they build a strong, agile, disciplined body and a focused mind.

What does a beginner Wushu class look like?

No experience is needed — everyone starts with fundamentals. A typical beginner session follows a clear structure:

  1. Warm-up and joint mobility — light running, skipping and joint rotations to prepare the body.
  2. Stretching — dynamic leg swings and progressive flexibility work. Nobody is expected to do the splits on day one.
  3. Basic stances — horse stance (mabu), bow stance (gongbu), drop stance (pubu), empty stance (xubu) and resting stance (xiebu). These are the alphabet of Wushu.
  4. Basic kicks and hand techniques — straight kicks, front kicks, punches and palm strikes, done slowly first and then with speed.
  5. Footwork and combinations — linking stances and techniques into short sequences.
  6. Conditioning and cool-down — core, leg strength and gentle stretching.

Once the basics are stable, students learn their first complete form. Weapons come later, after the body has the strength and control to handle them safely.

Do you need to be flexible to start?

No. Flexibility is something Wushu builds, not something it requires. Beginners often arrive unable to touch their toes. With regular, sensible stretching most students improve steadily over months. The key is patience: forcing flexibility causes injuries. Who is Wushu good for?

  • Kids — Wushu's jumps, kicks and games build coordination and burn energy in a structured way. See Wushu for kids.
  • Students and teens — a demanding, athletic art with a clear competition pathway.
  • Athletes — Wushu develops explosive power, balance and body awareness that transfer to other sports.
  • Adults — beginners of any age can learn the fundamentals and forms at a sensible pace.

Wushu vs Kungfu vs Karate

Wushu is excellent for flexibility, speed and competition foundation. Kungfu leans more towards traditional styles and their philosophy, and Karate follows a structured Japanese belt system. If you are unsure which suits you, read our Kungfu vs Karate vs Wushu comparison.
