Teachers' Day Lesson: A Good Coach Shapes Character

This Teachers' Day, a salute to every coach and guru — because technique fades, but the character a good teacher builds stays for life.

Teachers' Day Lesson: A Good Coach Shapes Character

Quick Answer

A good coach teaches technique; a great one shapes character — modelling discipline, correcting with respect, and believing in students before they believe in themselves. That is the real syllabus.

Happy Teachers' Day. Days like this always make me think about my own coaches — and about what they actually gave me.

It was never just technique

I can trace my competition results to specific corrections from specific teachers. But I can trace my character to something else: watching them arrive early, treat beginners with patience, and stay calm when shouting would have been easier.

Coaches teach by being watched

Children copy what teachers are, not just what they say. A coach who models discipline, humility and respect installs those qualities silently, class after class. A coach who shouts, mocks or cuts corners teaches those lessons too.

The belief transfer

Many students remember the day a teacher believed in them before they believed in themselves. That transferred belief is often the seed of everything that follows — the first competition entered, the first difficult exam attempted, the first time a child thinks "maybe I can".

What makes a good martial arts coach?

  • Technical knowledge — real expertise and qualifications in the art.
  • Teaching skill — breaking techniques into steps, explaining clearly, correcting specifically.
  • Patience — with beginners, with mistakes, with slow progress.
  • Consistency — the same standards and the same warmth every class.
  • Fairness — no favourites; every child is seen.
  • Safety focus — progressions, supervision, controlled contact.
  • Character — living the values they teach.

How do I evaluate a coach before joining?

Watch a normal class. Notice how the coach speaks to children, how they correct, whether they use names, how they respond to a mistake or a crying child. Ask about their background and approach. Read why your child needs a coach, not just a class and questions to ask before joining.

The guru tradition

India has a long tradition of honouring the guru — the teacher who shapes not just skills but the whole person. Martial arts carries a similar tradition: the bond between teacher and student, built on respect in both directions. Good coaching keeps that tradition alive while leaving behind outdated habits like harsh punishment. Read the real meaning of the bow.

To Chennai's teachers

To every school teacher, tuition teacher and sports coach shaping this city's children — from all of us at Spartacus, thank you. Your work is often invisible until it walks out into the world, standing tall.

A note for parents

Choose your child's teachers carefully — they matter more than syllabuses. And thank them. A short message telling a teacher what difference they made means more than you might think.
