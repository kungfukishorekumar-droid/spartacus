What Parents Should Ask Before Joining a Martial Arts Class

Choosing a martial arts class for your child? Ask these questions first — a good academy will welcome every one of them.

Spartacus Martial ArtsBy Kishore Kumar18 Jul 2020 · 07:45 AMUpdated 13 Oct 20265 min read
What Parents Should Ask Before Joining a Martial Arts Class

Quick Answer

Ask about the coach's qualifications, class sizes, how beginners are protected, how discipline is handled, and whether you can watch a class. A good academy answers all five happily.

As a coach, I respect the parents who interview me before enrolling their child. Asking questions is not rude — it is responsible. Here are the questions careful parents ask, what a good answer sounds like, and what should make you cautious. Use them with any academy, including ours.

1. "Who teaches, and what are their credentials?"

Ask about the coach's own training, competition history, coaching certification and how long they have taught children. Also ask who teaches when the head coach is away.

A good answer is specific and proud. A warning sign is vagueness, or a class mostly run by senior students without supervision.

2. "How do you keep beginners safe?"

Listen for: proper warm-ups, no early sparring, gradual progression, controlled partner work, protective gear when contact begins, and supervision at all times. Safety should sound like a system, not a promise. Read what makes a martial arts academy safe for children.

3. "How large are the classes, and how are ages grouped?"

A six-year-old should not drill next to a sixteen-year-old. Age and level grouping shows the academy takes development seriously. Ask how many students a coach handles at once — it determines how often your child gets corrected.

4. "How do you handle discipline?"

The right answer involves calm correction, clear boundaries and a chance to try again — never humiliation, never fear, never physical punishment.

5. "Can I watch a class first?"

Any academy proud of its culture says yes instantly. Watch a normal class, not just a demonstration. We welcome parents to observe.

6. "What will my child learn in the first three months?"

A good academy has a clear syllabus and can tell you what a beginner will be able to do after 90 days — basic stances, techniques, a first form, improved coordination. "We'll see" is not a plan.

7. "How do belt promotions work?"

Ask how often gradings happen, what is tested, and whether promotions are earned or automatic. Merit-based promotions protect your child's foundation. Read a parent's guide to belt progress.

8. "What are all the costs?"

Monthly fee, admission, uniform, gear, grading fees, competition costs. Ask for all of them upfront. Read our honest fees guide.

9. "What happens if we need a break?"

Exams, illness, travel and family events happen. Ask how pauses and restarts are handled, and whether fees are flexible in those situations.

10. "How will you keep me updated?"

Good academies talk to parents — about progress, attendance and any concerns. Ask how and how often.

Is a cheaper class a red flag?

Not necessarily. A low fee might reflect a small venue or a new academy. But do not choose on price alone; choose on coaching quality and safety, then compare cost. The cheapest class becomes expensive if your child gets injured or quits within two months.

Should my child try before enrolling?

Always. A trial shows you how your child responds to the coach, the class and the art. Watch their face in the last ten minutes — that tells you more than any brochure.
