Parent Checklist Before Choosing Martial Arts in Chennai

Print this checklist before visiting academies in Chennai: coach, safety, class, culture, logistics. Ten checks, one confident decision.

Parent Checklist Before Choosing Martial Arts in Chennai

Quick Answer

The ten-point check: coach credentials, supervision, warm-ups, age grouping, sparring policy, discipline style, trial availability, class size, honest fees, and your child's face after class. Verify, don't assume.

Chennai has many martial arts options — from Perambur to Anna Nagar, Villivakkam to Kilpauk — and quality varies a lot. Carry this checklist when you visit academies and you will make a confident decision. Use it on every academy, including ours.

The coach checks

  • ☐ Verifiable credentials and achievements in the art being taught
  • ☐ The head coach actually teaches the classes
  • ☐ Uses children's names and corrects individually
  • ☐ Calm discipline — no humiliation, shouting or fear tactics
  • ☐ Explains why, not just what

The safety checks

  • ☐ A full warm-up every session
  • ☐ Constant supervision; mats in good condition
  • ☐ Sparring only after months, with gear, and always optional
  • ☐ Partners matched by age, size and experience
  • ☐ Asks about your child's health and injuries
  • ☐ Drinking water, ventilation and space

Read what makes a martial arts academy safe for children.

The class checks

  • ☐ Age and level grouping — no six-year-olds drilling with teenagers
  • ☐ Class size that allows individual attention
  • ☐ A clear structure: warm-up, technique, practice, cool-down
  • ☐ A mix of focus and fun
  • ☐ A trial class offered without pressure

The culture checks

  • ☐ Children are respectful and happy, not anxious
  • ☐ Senior students help beginners
  • ☐ Values are taught, not just techniques
  • ☐ Belt promotions are earned, not automatic

The logistics checks

  • ☐ Travel time works in real evening traffic
  • ☐ Batch timings fit school and tuition — including exam months
  • ☐ All costs explained upfront: fee, admission, uniform, gear, grading
  • ☐ Clear policy on breaks and restarts
  • ☐ Easy communication with the coach

The final check

☐ Your child's face after the trial. Tired and glowing is a yes.

What is the biggest red flag?

A culture of fear — coaches who shout at, mock or physically punish children, or who push young beginners into hard sparring. Technique can be improved; a harmful culture rarely changes.

Are fees a quality signal?

Not reliably. A high fee does not guarantee good coaching, and a modest fee does not mean poor quality. Judge coaching, safety and culture first, then consider whether the fee is fair for what is offered.

How many academies should we visit?

Two or three is usually enough to compare meaningfully. Watch a normal class at each, not only a demonstration.
